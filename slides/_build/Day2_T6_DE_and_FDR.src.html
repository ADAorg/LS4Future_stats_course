Debrief of section A
Every pair finds around 500 "significant" genes, none of them real. That's 5% of 10,000, exactly as Day 1 predicted. A paper reporting "487 genes changed at p < 0.05" from such an experiment would be reporting noise. The rest of the session: how to choose a list you can trust.

Three rules for choosing your hits.

Raw p < 0.05

Controls errors per gene. With 10,000 null genes: ~500 false hits. Fine for one planned test; useless for a screen.

Bonferroni

p < 0.05 / number of tests. Controls the chance of even one false hit (family-wise error). Very strict: misses many real genes.

Benjamini–Hochberg (BH)

Controls the false discovery rate: the expected fraction of false hits in your list. DESeq2's padj.

The lecture · how each rule draws its line
A small world: 200 genes, 30 truly changed. Step 1: sort all p-values from smallest to largest; we show the first 70. Step 2: raw p < 0.05 calls everything under the flat line, including a pile of grey (null) genes. Step 3: Bonferroni's line is 0.05/200, almost on the floor: only the strongest true genes pass. Step 4: BH's line rises with rank (0.05 × k/200): you go along the sorted list and keep everything up to the last gene that sits under the line. Most true genes are in, and only a few grey ones: about 5% of the list.

Always plot this before trusting a DE analysis. A healthy histogram is a flat floor plus a spike at zero. A hump in the middle or a pile near 1 suggests a problem: a wrong model, unmodelled batch, or filtering issues. Here the floor says roughly two thirds of the genes are unchanged by dexamethasone.

The winner's curse (Day 1 · Topic 5) · discussion question 2
We split the airway experiment in two: DESeq2 on 2 cell lines ("discovery") and, separately, on the other 2 ("replication"). Each dot is a gene: its log2 fold change in discovery (x) and in replication (y). The diagonal is "replicates perfectly"; the shaded quadrants are genes whose direction flipped (up in discovery, down in replication, or the reverse). Walk through the gene buttons left to right. All genes: a cloud around the diagonal, no bias. Significant in discovery: their discovery fold changes are on average larger than the same genes' replication fold changes. Top 50 by fold change: much larger gap. Low-expression hits: the most inflated, because their estimates are the noisiest. Selection on a noisy estimate picks lucky overestimates: Day 1's winner's curse, gene by gene. Practical consequences: expect smaller effects and some failures in qPCR follow-up; DESeq2's lfcShrink exists for exactly this reason. The replication rate here understates true replication because 2 cell lines have little power.

"padj = 0.03": a 3% chance of being false?

🎓 Tutor prompt from the notebook, a known failure point:

DESeq2 gives my favourite gene an adjusted p-value (padj) of 0.03. What is the probability that this gene is a false positive?

A good answer says:

  • BH controls the false discovery rate of the whole list at the chosen cutoff
  • it isn't a per-gene probability of being false
  • genes near the cutoff are more likely false than those at the top
  • "there is a 3% chance this gene is a false positive"

Day 2 in six lines.

  1. t-test, ANOVA, correlation: one linear model.
  2. "Adjusted for" = compared at the same value; adjust for confounders, not mediators.
  3. Read the residuals before the p-values.
  4. Yes/no outcomes: odds ratios, which are not risk ratios when outcomes are common.
  5. Biological counts are overdispersed: negative binomial.
  6. 20,000 tests: control the FDR, respect the design, and distrust the top of the list.
